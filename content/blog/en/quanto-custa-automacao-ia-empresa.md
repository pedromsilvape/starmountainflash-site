**Short answer:** a simple automation costs between €800 and €1,200. With AI, or with two or three connected processes, it comes to between €1,200 and €2,500. A custom system, with several processes and its own database, starts at €3,500. Once it's running, allow €10 to €60 a month for tools and, if you want ongoing support, between €49 and €399 a month. These are the prices I charge in 2026.

| Type | Example | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Simple automation | A website enquiry goes into the CRM and gets an automatic reply | €800 to €1,200 |
| With AI or 2 to 3 workflows | Supplier invoices read and entered into a spreadsheet | €1,200 to €2,500 |
| Custom system | Several processes, a database and its own dashboard | from €3,500 |
| Monthly support | Monitoring, adjustments and fixes | €49 to €399/month |

## Why do two similar automations have different prices?

- **Number of tools.** Connecting the form to email is one step. Connecting the form, the CRM, the invoicing software and the calendar is four, and each connection has to be tested.
- **Official integration or not.** If the software has a ready-made connection (API), the automation is quicker to build. If it doesn't, you need a robot that operates the software the way a person would, which takes more work and needs more maintenance.
- **Fixed rules or AI.** "If the email comes from this address, save it in this folder" is a rule. "Read this PDF and work out who the supplier is and the total" needs AI and testing with real documents.
- **Exceptions.** The price goes up with the cases that break the rule: crooked scanned invoices, customers who reply outside the format, missing data.
- **Human review.** When a mistake costs money, the automation prepares the work and someone approves it before it moves on. That step has to be designed too.

## What costs remain once the automation is built?

- **Automation platform.** [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing) in the cloud costs €20 a month on annual billing, for 2,500 executions. Make has entry plans in the same range. In many cases I use the accounts the business already has.
- **AI by usage.** Language models charge per document or message processed. For a small business with normal volume, that's usually a few euros a month.
- **Support.** Tools change, passwords expire and document formats change. Without someone keeping watch, an automation can stop without anyone noticing.

## How do you know if an automation pays off?

Do this sum: **hours per week × cost of one hour of that person's time × 46 working weeks**.

An example with round numbers: a task that takes 5 hours a week from someone whose hour costs the business €15.

- Current cost per year: 5 × 15 × 46 = **€3,450**
- An automation costing €1,600, with support at €85 a month (€1,020 a year)
- In year 1, the business keeps **€830**. From year 2, **€2,430** a year.

If the task takes 30 minutes a week, the sum doesn't add up, and I'll tell you so during the assessment. In a [GfK survey for Cegid](https://www.santander.pt/salto/automacao-nas-pmes) of 650 Portuguese small and medium businesses, 69% of those that have automation as a priority said they want to automate repetitive tasks that take up around five hours a day per employee. That's where the sum tends to add up quickly.

## Examples of automations by price band

- **Simple:** enquiries from the website and email go into the CRM with source and date; automatic booking reminders; a weekly report put together and sent at the same time every week.
- **With AI:** supplier invoices read and entered into Excel; emails sorted by subject; replies prepared for questions that keep coming up.
- **Several workflows or a custom system:** a new client onboarding that creates folders, records and tasks; the same information written once and updated across every tool.

## Why do I run a diagnosis before giving a price?

Without knowing how long the task takes today, any price is a guess, and the sum above can't be done. In the [StarMap Method](/en/starmap), your team logs its tasks for one week and I cross-check that data with the tools you already use. At the end you get a report with what is worth automating, what isn't and the estimated return, presented in a meeting.

## What to ask before accepting an automation quote

- Who owns the automation's account and access: my business or the supplier?
- What happens when a connected program changes or stops responding?
- Are the monthly tool costs included or separate?
- Has it been tested with real cases, or only with demo examples?
- Is there a human review in the steps where a mistake costs money?
